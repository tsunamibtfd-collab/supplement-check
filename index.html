What “graded by the strength of the research” means

Strong

Consistent benefit across many randomised controlled trials in people, backed by expert consensus such as the IOC 2018 statement and the Australian Institute of Sport framework.

Moderate

Benefit in several human trials, but only in some situations (certain sports, or people who are low in a nutrient), or results vary between studies.

Emerging

Small, short or mixed human trials. Plausible but not proven, and the report says so plainly.

When food, sleep or movement beats a pill

  • Sleep7–9 hours does more for recovery, mood and focus than anything in a bottle.
  • Exercise for moodRegular activity has strong evidence for mood and stress; most mood supplements are emerging.
  • Protein from foodSpreading 20–40 g across meals usually beats a shake, for less money.
  • DaylightMorning light sets your body clock for better sleep at night.

Safety checks run on every report

  • InteractionsNine medicine groups, including blood thinners, antidepressants, blood pressure, thyroid and diabetes medicines.
  • ContraindicationsNine conditions, plus pregnancy and breastfeeding. Options that don't fit are left out and the reason is shown.
  • Upper limitsCaffeine 400 mg a day for adults and 300 mg in pregnancy (Health Canada), vitamin D 4,000 IU, supplemental magnesium 350 mg.
  • Under 18No supplement suggestions; food and activity guidance only.
  • Low moodA support message and the 9-8-8 crisis line come first when answers suggest it.
  • Drug-tested athletesAdvice to use only batch-tested products (NSF Certified for Sport, Informed Sport).
